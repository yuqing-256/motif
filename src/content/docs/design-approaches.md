Motif has one unified design system with two complementary design approaches. They are ways of
making design decisions, not separate themes, component libraries, or mutually exclusive interface
modes.

## Hugin — Editorial Design Approach

Hugin focuses on content expression, presentation, reading, and communication. Its primary concerns
include:

- Content hierarchy and narrative structure.
- Typography and reading experience.
- Page composition and reading rhythm.
- Visual emphasis and information presentation.
- Editorial presentation and distribution.

Hugin guides how content is expressed and experienced.

## Munin — Functional Design Approach

Munin focuses on content creation, organization, management, and operation. Its primary concerns
include:

- Information architecture and navigation.
- Workspaces and operational workflows.
- Controls and interaction states.
- Feedback and system status.
- Task efficiency and functional clarity.

Munin guides how users interact with information and accomplish tasks.

## Relationship between the approaches

Hugin and Munin are complementary. They may contribute to the same product, page, or interface.

Their relative emphasis depends on the purpose of the experience. An editorial page may rely
primarily on Hugin while using Munin for navigation and interaction. A functional workspace may rely
primarily on Munin while using Hugin to present content clearly.

Neither approach is restricted to a particular platform or appearance mode.

## What the approaches are not

- They are not themes or appearance modes.
- They are not separate component libraries.
- They are not mutually exclusive interface modes.
- They are not a classification that every page or component must fall into.
